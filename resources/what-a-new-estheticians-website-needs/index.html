Building Your First Esthetician Website: What You Need

You've spent countless hours learning about skin, perfecting your technique, and developing your approach to client care. Now it's time to introduce your business to the world.

As a licensed esthetician, former spa owner, and Creative Developer, I've experienced this process from both sides. I understand the challenges of starting an aesthetics business, as well as the technical and design decisions that go into creating its online presence.

Here is where I would focus your time and investment when getting started.

Make It Easy to Book

When someone visits your website, they should be able to quickly understand what you offer, where you're located, and how to book an appointment.

People appreciate convenience. They want to explore your services, check availability, and book an appointment without having to make a phone call or wait for a response.

When I opened my spa, I chose Acuity as my booking system. I was looking for something budget-friendly, but I also wanted the experience to look professional and work well from day one. As a developer, I spent considerable time customizing the experience so it felt like a natural extension of my business.

Both design and functionality mattered to me.

It's equally important to consider the mobile experience. Someone should be able to explore your services and complete the entire booking process from their phone without difficulty.

Start With a Focused Service Menu

One of the most common challenges I see with new estheticians is trying to offer too much.

It's understandable. When you're starting out, you want to appeal to as many potential clients as possible. But offering everything can make it harder for clients to understand what sets you apart.

For many estheticians, their interests begin to take shape in school. Perhaps you loved working with brows, lashes, corrective skincare, or performing relaxing facial treatments.

Build around what you enjoy and do well. Your service menu can always evolve as your business grows.

When developing service menus for wellness spas, I've found that creating a cohesive menu that works both online and in print helps simplify the experience for clients.

Each service should clearly communicate:

  • What the treatment involves
  • Who it may benefit
  • Treatment duration
  • Pricing
  • What the client can expect

Keep descriptions informative without overwhelming someone with technical terminology.

A thoughtfully curated service menu can communicate more confidence and expertise than an extensive list of treatments.

Price With Purpose

Pricing your services is one of the more challenging decisions when starting your own aesthetics business.

Research your local market, understand your demographic, and consider the cost of delivering each treatment. Your pricing should reflect your training, products, time, overhead, and the experience you provide.

You don't need to compete by being the least expensive esthetician in your area.

Consider creating introductory treatments, complementary service bundles, or packages for a series of appointments. These can provide value to clients without permanently lowering the perceived value of your services.

There is no universal pricing structure. What works in one market may not work in another.

Think Beyond the Website

Your website is the front door to your business, but the experience doesn't end when someone books an appointment.

From scheduling and payment to intake forms, appointment reminders, and follow-up communication, these systems should work together to create a cohesive client experience.

When evaluating software for your business, consider whether it supports:

  • Online scheduling
  • Secure payment processing
  • Appointment confirmations and reminders
  • Client intake and consent forms
  • Cancellation and deposit policies
  • Client communication and follow-up

When you're starting out, you don't necessarily need the most expensive software or the platform with the most features. You need tools that work well together, are easy for you to manage, and are intuitive for your clients to use.

Intake forms are particularly easy to overlook. Clients appreciate a process that is straightforward, convenient, and respectful of their time.

I've explored this further in Why Clients Put Off Your Intake Forms and What Makes a Great Intake Experience?

Thinking through these details early can save you time and create a more professional experience as your business grows.

Building Trust Before You Have an Established Clientele

One of the biggest challenges of opening my own spa was building a clientele from the ground up. Without years of reviews or an established reputation, every interaction mattered.

Your website is often the first of those interactions.

Aesthetics is a personal industry. Clients are trusting you with their skin, their appearance, and sometimes their insecurities. They want to feel comfortable with the person providing their care.

Give potential clients an opportunity to get to know you.

Include a professional photograph of yourself, introduce your background and credentials, share authentic photographs of your treatment space, and explain your approach to skincare.

Consider including information about what someone can expect during their first appointment. Something that feels routine to you may be entirely unfamiliar to a new client.

As you begin gathering testimonials, incorporate them into your website with your clients' permission. You don't need dozens of reviews to start building credibility.

The design itself also plays a role. A thoughtful, uncluttered layout with plenty of white space can begin to communicate the same sense of care, professionalism, and calm that you want clients to experience in your treatment room.

Help Local Clients Find You

A beautiful website isn't particularly useful if potential clients can't find it.

Make your business location, contact information, and hours of operation easy to find. If you're working within a salon, wellness center, or private studio, provide clear information about where clients should go when they arrive.

If your business is eligible, establish a Google Business Profile and keep your information consistent across your website, booking platform, and business listings.

Naturally incorporating your location throughout your website can also help search engines understand the geographic area you serve.

You don't need an elaborate digital marketing strategy on your first day, but establishing these foundations early is worthwhile.

Create a Brand That Feels Like You

Being in aesthetics naturally made me thoughtful about the visual identity of my own spa. I wanted the colors, typography, and overall experience to reflect the environment I was creating for my clients.

Your branding doesn't have to be elaborate. Start with a few foundational elements:

  • A thoughtfully designed logo
  • A cohesive color palette
  • Consistent typography
  • Photography that reflects your style
  • A recognizable tone of communication

Carry those elements throughout your website, service menu, booking page, printed materials, and client communications.

Branding is more than a logo. It's the familiarity clients begin to associate with your business through repeated, consistent experiences.

And while I personally gravitate toward clean, airy designs for aesthetics and wellness businesses, your visual identity should ultimately reflect you and the clientele you want to attract.

Build for Where You Are Today

You don't need to launch with an elaborate website, an extensive service menu, or every piece of software available to the aesthetics industry.

Start with a strong foundation. Make it easy for clients to understand what you offer, feel comfortable choosing you, and book an appointment.

As your clientele grows and you better understand the needs of your business, your website and digital systems can grow with you.

A beautiful website is only one part of creating a thoughtful, functional client experience. How everything works together is equally important.

If you're establishing your aesthetics business or reconsidering how your existing digital systems work together, explore my website design services or tell me what you're working on.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many pages does a new esthetician's website need?

A homepage, an About page, a service menu, and a contact or booking page are a good starting point. Additional pages can be introduced as your business grows.

Do I need online booking as a new esthetician?

I strongly recommend it. Online booking allows clients to schedule appointments at their convenience while reducing the time you spend managing appointments manually.

Should I display my service prices online?

Yes, whenever possible. Transparent pricing helps clients make informed decisions and reduces uncertainty before booking.

Do I need a custom website when I'm just starting out?

Not necessarily. The priority is having a professional, functional online presence that reflects your business. Choose an approach that fits your budget, technical needs, and plans for growth.

When should I invest in more advanced software?

When your existing systems no longer meet the needs of your business or create unnecessary administrative work. Start with the essentials and expand intentionally.
