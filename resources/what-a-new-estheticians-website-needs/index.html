What a New Esthetician's Website Needs

A new esthetician's website has one job before anything else: let a stranger see what you offer and book it. Nobody knows you yet, so the site has to do some of the work a reputation would do later.

I ran my own spa, and I've built and maintained websites for several wellness spas. The sites that work tend to be simpler than their owners expect. Here is what I'd put on yours, in the order I'd think about it.

What Should a New Esthetician's Website Let Someone Do?

It should let someone see your services and book one, quickly. This is my opinion from what I see today: you have to capture attention fast. People don't spend long on websites anymore. They want to find your service and get it booked.

That makes online booking close to essential. People don't love picking up the phone these days.

When I opened my spa, I used Acuity as my booking system. I was looking for something budget-friendly, but I still wanted the experience to work well and look good from day one. Because I'm a developer, I put a lot of time into setting it up. Pleasant and functional both mattered to me, and a booking page that feels clumsy makes a new business look less sure of itself.

Why Does a Smaller Service Menu Work Better?

A smaller menu is easier for a client to understand in a few seconds. The most common problem I see is an esthetician who offers too many services and hasn't decided what she loves and does well.

It's an easy trap. When you're new, you want to say yes to everything. But a long menu with no focus makes a website confusing, and a confusing website looks amateur.

Your niche can be obvious: lashes, makeup, brows only, or relaxation and massage. For many people it starts to show up in school, in what you loved doing and what you didn't. Build around the things you love and do well.

Your booking system needs a list of services anyway. The question is how long that list should be. When I developed a cohesive menu for wellness spas I worked with, one that lived on the website and also worked in print, clients found it less confusing.

How Should a New Esthetician Price Services?

Price for your market, and be honest that you're new. You don't want to undercut yourself, but you also shouldn't price as if you have ten years of reviews.

Know your market, your demographic, and your location. Then make the pricing appealing. You might bundle a few services together, or offer a discount on a series. I'm not putting dollar figures here, because the right numbers depend on where you work.

What Tools Sit Behind the Website?

You need three working pieces: a booking system, a way to take payment, and a way to reach your clients with email or text reminders. Check that the system you choose covers all three before you commit.

Forms come next, and they're easy to overlook. People hate forms. They want to finish them quickly and move on, so the easier you make it, the better. I've written about why clients put forms off in Why Clients Put Off Your Intake Forms, and about the bigger picture in What Makes a Great Intake Experience?

How Does a New Esthetician Build Trust Before She Has a Reputation?

Get testimonials early, and put them on your site as soon as you have them. They add trust that you can't create by describing yourself.

I'll be honest about my own spa. My clients did book. I'm still not sure how they came to trust me, or whether I fully earned it. Gathering clients as a new esthetician is hard, and the surface industry is hard.

What I can say is what I've seen work on the design side. A simple, clean, airy website, with plenty of white space, fits the feeling most people look for in a spa or wellness center. That's my opinion, but I hold it firmly.

What Does Branding Mean for a New Esthetician?

It means a logo, a set of brand colors, and a look that shows your style. Working in aesthetics meant I cared about aesthetics, so I chose a color palette that felt like me.

Then use it everywhere: your website, your menu, your booking page, your reminders. Repetition is what turns a logo and a few colors into a brand.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many services should a new esthetician list?

Few enough that a client understands your focus in a few seconds. There isn't one right number. It depends on your niche and what you're good at.

Should a new esthetician discount to get clients?

I'd price fairly for your market and be honest that you're new. Bundles or a discount on a series of visits usually make more sense than lowering every price.

What does a new esthetician's website need first?

A clear list of services, online booking, a way to take payment, and email or text reminders.

When should I add testimonials?

As soon as you have them. Early testimonials help a new esthetician build trust.

Does an esthetician's website need to look fancy?

No. It needs to look clean and work well. A simple, airy layout with white space fits what most people expect from a spa or wellness center.
